Yaws is a tropical skin disease caused by the bacterium Treponema pallidum subsp. pertenue, which affects people in tropical regions like Ghana, and can lead to deformities without treatment. The disease is mostly found in children less than 15 years old and is transmitted by direct skin-to-skin contact with infectious lesions. Currently, it is treated with a single oral dose of 30mg/kg of Azithromycin. Diagnosis primarily relies on serological tests, with the WHO recommending the Dual Path Platform (DPP) Syphilis Screen and Confirm Assay. However, due to the high cost of DPP test kits, many countries depend on clinical diagnosis, which requires skilled health workers for accuracy. Given that H. ducreyi has been reported to cause lesions clinically resembling those of yaws, this overlap can hinder accurate diagnosis and may potentially influence the treatment outcomes for yaws. Our findings are important because they show how multiple organisms can complicate disease control, highlight the need for better diagnostic tools, and possibly adjust treatments to ensure everyone gets better. This could help health workers improve care and support the global effort to eliminate yaws, benefiting both local communities and global health.
