In the Amazon region, certain bats known as vampire bats are the main reservoir for the rabies virus. They can transmit the disease to humans through bites. Although rare, human rabies is almost always fatal and can go unnoticed, particularly in isolated areas. Diagnosis is made all the more difficult by the fact that the disease can take unusual forms and that certain populations, such as gold miners, are also exposed to toxic substances that can cause neurological disorders.
This study reports three cases that occurred in 2024 among gold miners living in an isolated mining camp in French Guiana. These patients developed almost simultaneously rapid paralysis of both legs, followed by severe brain damage leading to death. Two of them had been bitten by bats about two weeks earlier. Neurological examinations showed damage to the spinal cord resembling certain forms of rabies, but without the usual signs of the disease, which delayed diagnosis. Rabies was finally confirmed by biological tests before and after death.
These cases show that in the Amazon region, any unusual neurological damage should raise suspicion of rabies transmitted by bats, in order to improve diagnosis and treatment.
