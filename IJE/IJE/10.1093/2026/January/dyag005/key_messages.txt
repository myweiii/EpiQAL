Prior studies suggest that sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine (SP)—the standard-of-care drug used for the intermittent preventive treatment of malaria in pregnancy (IPTp)—may have activity against certain sexually transmitted and reproductive tract infections (STIs/RTIs). In a randomized trial comparing monthly IPTp with SP versus dihydroartemisinin–piperaquine, SP was associated with lower prevalences of Chlamydia trachomatis and Group B Streptococcus at delivery; these were associated with increased risks of preterm delivery and term low birthweight, respectively. IPTp-SP may offer benefits in addition to its antimalarial activity through its action against certain STIs/RTIs, which may contribute to improved birth outcomes.
