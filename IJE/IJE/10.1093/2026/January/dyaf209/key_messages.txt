We reviewed the results from the studies conducted in the Puberty Cohort, within the Danish National Birth Cohort (DNBC), over the last 10 years addressing the effect of prenatal and childhood biological and psychosocial factors on puberty. The strongest associations with earlier puberty were observed for maternal age at menarche, childhood overweight and obesity, maternal smoking in pregnancy, several maternal gestational diseases, and psychosocial exposures such as exposure to parental divorce. Descriptive data from the DNBC Puberty Cohort indicated an ongoing decline in age at puberty among Danish girls and boys, which is of concern due to its associations with later physiological and psychiatric morbidities.
