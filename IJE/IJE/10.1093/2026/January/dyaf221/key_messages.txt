We tested the hypothesis that health workers in Zimbabwe would report a higher prevalence of common mental disorders (CMD) symptoms and intimate partner violence (IPV) via audio computer-assisted self-interviewing (ACASI) than via paper-based self-administered questionnaires (SAQ) handed in to a research assistant. We found that, in a cluster-randomized trial of ACASI versus SAQ, ACASI was associated with increased prevalence of CMD symptoms and IPV. ACASI is recommended for screening for CMD symptoms and IPV because studies that use SAQ may underestimate the prevalence of these conditions and fail to detect people who would benefit from referral.
