This study investigated the impact of a nationwide gastric cancer screening program in South Korea on gastric cancer incidence in Seoul, using a quasi-experimental design. The gastric cancer screening program initially increased gastric cancer incidence in Seoul, peaking in the 10th year before gradually declining and eventually reversing into a potential reduction effect. These findings show that new screening programs can temporarily inflate cancer incidence, so health services must prepare for an early surge in demand, and long-term data should be used for assessing the potential preventive impact.
