We investigated the proportion of Mycobacterium tuberculosis genetically linked index–household contact (HHC) patient pairs in household-based studies and examined whether this proportion varied with the community tuberculosis (TB) burden. Our analysis showed that, in low- and moderate-incidence-TB settings, ≥61% of index–HHC pairs were genetically linked, suggesting that within-household transmission predominates in these settings. These findings suggest that, in moderate- and low-TB-burden settings, the force of infection among HHCs exposed to TB patients at home is primarily driven by their index TB patient, reinforcing the validity of household-based studies conducted in these settings.
