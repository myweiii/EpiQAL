We tested whether using calendars of local events during household surveys and censuses can help ascertain more precisely the reported dates of recent deaths in settings in which civil registration and vital statistics systems are limited. Neither paper-based nor digital calendars improved the accuracy of reported dates of recent deaths, but paper-based calendars increased data completeness without increasing interview duration. Further improvements in the collection of data on recent household deaths are needed to better document the impact of crises such as epidemics, conflicts, and natural disasters on population health.
