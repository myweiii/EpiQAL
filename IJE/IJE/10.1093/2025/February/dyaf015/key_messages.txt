Guidelines suggest satisfying the well-defined intervention assumption is key for estimating causal effects, yet no studies have systematically evaluated how the assumption is addressed in research; we addressed this gap. Reviewing 146 observational studies published in top epidemiology journals, we identified nonadherence to guidelines regarding meeting the well-defined intervention assumption, with authors of studies that aimed to estimate the effects of hypothetical interventions rarely critically examining the assumption’s validity, let alone specifying well-defined interventions. Consistent with recent studies highlighting the need for improved clarity of research objectives and application of causal-inference approaches in epidemiology, we call for more careful treatment of the well-defined intervention assumption in epidemiological research.
