Few studies assess associations between trajectories of drinking and mortality; thus, we aimed to assess associations between long-term sex-specific drinking trajectories and all-cause and disease-specific mortality. Three long-term drinking trajectories for women and six for men were identified; heavy drinking during early adulthood for men irrespective of intake after age 40 years was associated with 20% higher all-cause and 35% higher cancer-specific mortality, while increasing alcohol intake with advancing age for women was associated with 20% higher cancer-specific mortality, compared with lifetime abstention. Our findings highlight the importance of avoiding higher levels of alcohol intake during the life course to reduce all-cause and cancer-specific mortality.
