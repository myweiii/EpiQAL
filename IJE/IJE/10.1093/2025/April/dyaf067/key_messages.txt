We sought to identify neonate DNA methylation changes associated with childhood asthma risk in a large US based cohort study. We discovered methylation changes, present at birth, associated with prospective childhood asthma diagnoses and some of these changes were sex specific. Our findings suggest methylation, at specific positions in the genome, could serve as a biomarker of later asthma diagnoses and also provide insights into biology, at the earliest stages of life, i.e. relevant to asthma risk.
