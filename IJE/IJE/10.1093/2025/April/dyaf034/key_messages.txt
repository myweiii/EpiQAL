This is the first study to uncover the contribution of violent deaths and various causes of death to life-expectancy changes in Mexico before, during, and after the COVID-19 pandemic. Since 2015, the upsurge in violence has slowed life-expectancy gains for both females and males in Mexico, offsetting potential improvements from other causes of death. Life-expectancy losses during the COVID-19 pandemic were significant, primarily driven by COVID-19 deaths, increased diabetes-related deaths, and other causes that were amenable to healthcare.
