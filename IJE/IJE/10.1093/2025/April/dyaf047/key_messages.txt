We examined how neighbourhood features—social, built, and natural environments—are associated with body mass index (BMI) in 165 Latin American cities and whether these associations are modified by individual socioeconomic position (SEP) and gender. The association between neighbourhood education and BMI differs between countries and gender whereas the associations with intersection density and greenness and the effect modifications by individual SEP are less clear. Our findings suggest that neighbourhood characteristics in cities of Latin America appear to influence BMI differently from those in the Global North.
