We aimed to investigate the association between congenital anomalies (CAs) and long-term mortality in individuals who survived to the age of 18 years. CAs were associated with premature adult mortality, with risks varying by severity and body system affected, particularly the cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, and genitourinary systems. Our findings suggest that adults with CAs may require specialized long-term surveillance and call for more research into the causal mechanisms by which congenital anomalies are associated with premature adult mortality.
