We conducted a population-based surveillance study to assess progress toward the Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS) 95–95–95 targets by age and gender among people living with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) in southern Uganda from 2013 to 2019. We found that viremia did not concentrate the most in the age and gender groups furthest from achieving 95–95–95 targets, but rather those groups achieving UNAIDS targets contributed the most to viremia and could therefore potentially contribute the most to onward transmission. Additional metrics beyond the 95–95–95 targets that count the contribution of subgroups to viraemia could help to identify subgroups driving HIV transmission and inform targeted treatment-as-prevention strategies.
