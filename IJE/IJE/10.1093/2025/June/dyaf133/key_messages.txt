We evaluated whether statins are associated with the risk of cancers of the endometrium, ovary, and cervix, both overall and within subgroups defined by type of statin, strength of statin, age, histology, and body mass index. Statin use was associated with reduced risk of endometrial cancer, with associations observed only for the lipophilic statins, and with similar associations by age groups and for type I and II endometrial cancer. No consistent associations were found for ovarian or cervical cancers. The robust inverse associations observed for endometrial cancer, evident across various subgroups defined by individual characteristics (e.g. age, body mass index) and tumor histology, suggest that statins represent a class of drugs that may have promise as non-cancer drugs that could be re-purposed for endometrial cancer prevention.
