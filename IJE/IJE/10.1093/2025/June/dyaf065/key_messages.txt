Omega-3 (n-3) and omega-6 (n-6) polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) are thought to have anti- and pro-inflammatory roles, respectively, but it is unclear whether these associations are causal. Contrary to current understanding, n-3 FAs are not associated with lower inflammatory marker levels [C-reactive protein (CRP) or glycoprotein acetyls]. We report that both n-3 and n-6 PUFAs are associated with higher GlycA and CRP levels. Our findings argue against the presence of a simple pro- and anti-inflammatory dichotomy regarding the function of n-6 and n-3 PUFAs, respectively, and suggest that n-3 supplementation alone may not reduce systemic inflammation.
