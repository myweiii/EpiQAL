We found that long-term exposure to fine particulate matter (PM 2.5 ) and traffic-related air pollutants, including nitrogen dioxide (NO 2 ), and carbon monoxide (CO), was associated with the incidence of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). Furthermore, compared with the participants exposed to low levels of air pollutants, the incidence of NAFLD was higher in areas with high levels of both PM 2.5 and NO 2 , low levels of PM 2.5 and high levels of CO, and high levels of both PM 2.5 and CO. The findings are particularly valuable for regions with a relatively high prevalence of NAFLD and high levels of air pollutants, such as Asia and developing countries.
