The increasing incidence of ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) of the breast is attributed to mammography screening, but the trend has so far been evaluated only descriptively. This is the first age-period-cohort modelling analysis of the incidence trend of DCIS before and after the implementation of an organised screening programme (northern Italy, 1992–2017). The observed strong increase in DCIS rates resulted from an increased detection through the screening programme and from an uptrend in the risk of diagnosis that can be attributed either to long-term changes in diagnostic scrutiny independent of the programme or to an increased exposure to risk factors.
