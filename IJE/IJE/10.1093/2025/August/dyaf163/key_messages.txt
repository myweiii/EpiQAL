The assessment of long-term risks associated with environmental exposures such as air pollution relies on observational analyses of cohort datasets that are prone to various confounding mechanisms. This study offers a comprehensive overview based on theoretical considerations and empirical findings, using as a case study an analysis of the UK Biobank cohort. The analysis of direct acyclic graphs (DAGs) and substantive results suggest various potential confounding mechanisms, particularly those related to unmeasured spatial differences in exposure levels and baseline risks, as well as individual-level lifestyle factors.
